# lypo brand film.

Runtime 2:10. For BlackHawk Visuals (Devin Martin).

**Word budget matters here.** At a natural conversational pace, roughly 130 words a minute, 2:10 gives you about 200 words of your own voice total. Everything below is written to that. If you add lines, cut lines. The most common way a film like this fails is by cramming.

| section | slot | words | spoken | fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the why | 0:00-0:25 | 58 | ~27s | tight, fine |
| who I am | 0:25-0:55 | 78 | ~36s | 6s over, see note |
| demos | 0:55-1:45 | 11 | ~5s | wide open, others talk here |
| the close | 1:45-2:10 | 48 | ~22s | fits, leaves the silent beat |
| **total** | **2:10** | **195** | **~90s** | 40s of breathing room |

That 40 seconds of non-speech is not slack, it is the film. Held shots, the pause before the end card, the reaction faces. Don't fill it.

---

## 0:00 - 0:25 | the why

*Memphis b-roll. Early morning. Empty barbershop chair. A church sign. Somebody's hand-lettered plate-sale flyer taped to a pole. Hold each shot longer than feels comfortable. No music yet, or something very sparse.*

**VO (you, not to camera):**

> I played three sports. Football, basketball, track.
>
> Sophomore year I got hurt.
>
> First time in my life I had to sit still.
>
> And when you sit still, you start noticing things.
>
> The barbershop down the street. The church. The lady selling plates out her kitchen.
>
> Every one of them had something worth showing.
>
> Not one of them had a way to show it.

*(58 words)*

**Why this works:** it earns the mission without ever saying "digital divide," which is a phrase that makes people's eyes glaze. The injury isn't the story, it's the thing that made you slow down enough to see the story. Let the shots of real Memphis storefronts do the arguing.

**Direction note:** this VO should sound like you're telling one person, not addressing a crowd. Record it sitting down, close mic, low energy. The temptation will be to perform it. Don't.

## 0:25 - 0:55 | who I am, what this is

*Cut to you on camera. Warm interior, window light, shallow depth of field. Not a studio. Somewhere that looks like it's actually in your city.*

**On camera:**

> Getting hurt took the thing I thought I was. It showed me what I actually am. Somebody who builds things for other people.
>
> First thing I made was a health app for clinics in Africa. That's when it clicked.
>
> So I built Lypo. You describe what you want, it builds you a real website. No code. No designer. Nobody charging you two thousand dollars.
>
> It's for the fundraiser with nowhere to donate. The barbershop with no way to book.

*(78 words, about 36 seconds spoken. Your slot is 30. Either let this section run to 0:58 and take the 6 seconds back from the demo montage, which has room, or cut "Nobody charging you two thousand dollars." Your call, but I'd keep the line and steal the time.)*

**Direction note:** the line "took the thing I thought I was" is the emotional center of the film. Don't rush it. Devin should get a take where you pause before it.

## 0:55 - 1:45 | the demos

*Real people, real places. This is the longest section and it should have the least of your voice in it.*

**Do not script the people.** Scripted testimonials are obvious in about four seconds and they'll undo everything the first minute built. Put Lypo in their hands, roll, and get out of the way.

**What to capture:**

- Hands on a phone, typing or talking into it. Tight.
- The face, not the screen, at the moment the site appears. This is the shot the whole film is built around. If you get one good version of it, you have your trailer.
- Screen recording cut in tight and fast. Two or three seconds per beat. Prompt typed, site appears, phone preview, published.
- The physical follow-through: the QR poster going up in a window, somebody scanning it.

**Questions to ask on camera** (their answers become the VO bed):

1. What are you trying to get done?
2. How long have you been meaning to make a website?
3. What stopped you before?
4. Say what just happened.
5. Who are you going to send this to first?

Question 3 gets you the problem stated in their words, which lands harder than you stating it. Question 5 gets you the ending.

**Your only VO in this section, laid under the montage, around 1:30:**

> Thirty seconds. That's it. That's the whole thing.

*(11 words)*

## 1:45 - 2:10 | the close

*Back to Memphis. Golden hour if you can get it. Wider than the opening shots. Same locations from the top of the film, now with sites live on phones in frame. Music resolves.*

**VO:**

> Langston Hughes asked what happens to a dream deferred.
>
> Around here, a lot of them just wait.
>
> Not because people don't have ideas. Because nobody ever handed them the tools.
>
> So here they are.
>
> If you can describe it, you can build it.

*(48 words)*

*Beat of silence. Then the card.*

**END CARD:**

```
lypo.dev
```

Ink on paper white. Sora, lowercase, with the flame-colored period. Hold four seconds in silence. No tagline under it, no social handles, no "sign up today." The line before it already did that work.

## alternates

Swap these in if a take isn't landing.

**Opening line, if you want it harder:**
> Sophomore year I got hurt and everything I thought I was went with it.

**Opening line, if you want it quieter:**
> I used to think I was an athlete.

**The Hughes moment, if the attribution feels stiff on camera:**
> There's a poem that asks what happens to a dream deferred. Around here, a lot of them just wait.

**Closing line alternates:**
> If you can say it, you can build it.
> You already have the idea. Now you have the tools.

Keep "if you can describe it, you can build it" unless something clearly beats it. It's doing three jobs at once: it describes the product literally, it's a promise, and it's short enough to remember.

## notes for Devin

**Music.** Nothing triumphant. The first minute should feel like early morning, not like a startup ad. Let the demo section be the only place energy lifts, and bring it back down for the close. If the music tells people how to feel, the film is over.

**Grade.** Warm. Your brand is ink on warm paper, not blue-and-black tech. Keep the film in that world so the end card doesn't feel like it belongs to a different piece.

**The b-roll is the argument.** Every storefront, flyer, and church sign in the first 25 seconds is evidence for the thing you're claiming. Get more of it than you think you need. Handwritten signs, taped-up flyers, cardboard prices, anything where you can see a person tried. That's the whole thesis in a shot.

**Age.** You're 17. Don't hide it and don't lead with it. If it comes up naturally in an interview take, keep it. It's more credible when a viewer notices it themselves than when the film points at it.

**Get more interview than you need.** Shoot 15 minutes of you talking to get the 45 seconds. The good take is usually the fourth one, after you've stopped trying to say it right.

**On the Hughes line:** it's one line, attributed, used as commentary, which is normal practice for a film like this. If you ever cut a paid ad version, or use more than that single line, have somebody check it first.
